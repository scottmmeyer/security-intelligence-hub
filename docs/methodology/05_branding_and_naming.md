# 05 — Branding and Naming

## Primary Recommendation

### Consensus Intelligence Investing (CII)

**Description:**  
A multi-layer investment methodology that uses professional analyst consensus as its primary signal, validates that consensus against business fundamentals and historical performance, and disciplines capital deployment through systematic portfolio construction. The result is a system where intelligence about market consensus is transformed into disciplined, high-conviction portfolio decisions.

**Strengths:**
- Precisely describes what the system does (consensus as input; intelligence as the process)
- "Intelligence" positions it as more than just signal-following — it implies synthesis, validation, and judgment
- Differentiates clearly from purely quantitative, purely fundamental, and purely consensus-following approaches
- Professional and scalable for future product positioning
- Memorable as an acronym (CII) for institutional audiences

**Weaknesses:**
- "Consensus" may imply following the crowd — needs the word "Intelligence" to carry the weight of distinction
- Less evocative than some alternatives; requires context to fully appreciate
- Academic-sounding for consumer audiences

**Suitability:** Excellent for internal governance, institutional investor discussions, advisor conversations, and product documentation. Strong foundation for commercialization.

## Alternate Names

### 1. Guided Conviction Investing

**Description:**  
An investment system that uses multi-source analyst intelligence to guide conviction formation, validates that conviction against fundamental evidence and historical proof, and translates conviction into disciplined position sizing and capital deployment.

**Strengths:**
- "Conviction" resonates strongly with investment professionals
- "Guided" implies systematic, structured process — not emotional or arbitrary
- Explains the CCL/HCA tier system naturally

**Weaknesses:**
- "Conviction" is overused in investment marketing
- Doesn't clearly differentiate from traditional active management

**Suitability:** Strong for advisor-facing materials; weaker for institutional positioning.

---

### 2. Multi-Layer Investment Intelligence (MLII)

**Description:**  
A systematic investment framework built on four distinct evidence layers — analyst consensus, fundamental validation, historical performance, and portfolio discipline — each contributing to a final deployment decision.

**Strengths:**
- Accurately describes the architecture
- "Multi-layer" communicates sophistication and depth
- Scalable — can absorb new layers as the system grows

**Weaknesses:**
- Verbose; harder to brand as a phrase
- "Multi-layer" is technical; less accessible to non-technical audiences

**Suitability:** Best for technical documentation and engineering-facing materials.

---

### 3. Signal Intelligence Platform (SIP)

**Description:**  
A platform that transforms raw market signals into structured investment intelligence by aggregating, validating, and deploying analyst consensus across a disciplined portfolio construction framework.

**Strengths:**
- "Platform" positions it as infrastructure, not just a tool
- Emphasizes the transformation from raw signal to structured intelligence
- "Signal Intelligence" has a technology-forward feel

**Weaknesses:**
- Platform implies software product more than investment methodology
- Overlaps with intelligence/defense industry "SigInt" terminology in some contexts

**Suitability:** Good for product branding; less suitable for investment philosophy documentation.
