# 01 — SIH Methodology Classification

## The Question

What category of investing does the Security Intelligence Hub represent?

---

## Candidates Evaluated

### Growth Investing
Seeks companies with above-average revenue and earnings growth. Accepts premium valuations.

**SIH overlap:** SIH considers revenue growth (FMP income_growth) and EPS growth as validation evidence. But SIH does not require growth as a primary selection criterion. A mature, cash-generative company with flat revenue but strong consensus can rank highly.

**Verdict:** Partial overlap — not the primary methodology.

### Value Investing
Seeks stocks trading below intrinsic value (P/E, P/B, DCF discounts). Contrarian by nature.

**SIH overlap:** SIH's Dislocation Detection identifies potentially mispriced situations (INTACT thesis + weak signals). But SIH does not screen on valuation multiples or compute intrinsic value. It does not buy things because they are cheap — it identifies dislocations relative to analyst consensus, not DCF models.

**Verdict:** Partial overlap at the margins — not the primary methodology.

### Momentum Investing
Buys stocks that have recently outperformed; sells those that have underperformed. Price-trend driven.

**SIH overlap:** SIH includes a Momentum component in CW-DAS (ESS direction convergence) and treats signal momentum as a modifier. But the primary selection driver is consensus quality, not price trend. SIH explicitly does not use price momentum as a standalone criterion.

**Verdict:** Momentum is a component modifier, not the methodology.

### Quality Investing
Selects companies with durable competitive advantages, high ROIC, strong cash flow, and low debt.

**SIH overlap:** SIH uses ROIC and FCF Yield as fundamental validation evidence (Phase 8.0B.1B.5). High-ROIC companies validate the thesis behind high-conviction signals. But quality metrics are validation evidence, not selection criteria. A low-ROIC company with strong consensus and replay backing can still rank highly.

**Verdict:** Quality is a validation layer, not the selection engine.

### Quantitative Investing
Uses mathematical models, factor exposure, and statistical relationships to select securities. Typically model-driven, low human judgment.

**SIH overlap:** SIH uses quantitative scoring (composite signal 0–5, CW-DAS 0–103, UCF 0–100). But SIH is explicitly designed with a human operator in the loop. The system is explainable, override-friendly, and advisory — not autonomous. Scores are transparent inputs to human decisions, not black-box outputs.

**Verdict:** SIH uses quant tools but is not quant methodology in the traditional sense.

### Analyst Consensus Investing
Follows sell-side analyst ratings and price targets as the primary selection signal.

**SIH overlap:** Analyst consensus is SIH's primary signal layer (ESS, Zacks, Danelfin, Yahoo ABR). This is the closest single-category match. But SIH does not follow consensus blindly — it validates consensus against fundamentals and historical performance, then disciplines deployment through portfolio construction.

**Verdict:** Strong overlap — but SIH adds validation layers that pure consensus-following lacks.

### Multi-Factor Investing
Combines multiple return factors (value, momentum, quality, size, low-volatility). Academic factor-based.

**SIH overlap:** SIH combines multiple evidence types. But the factors are not academically derived factor premiums — they are practitioner signals organized into a layered validation hierarchy. SIH does not tilt toward a factor portfolio. It builds a concentrated conviction portfolio.

**Verdict:** Similar structure, different philosophy. SIH is not factor investing.

---

## Determination: SIH Is a Distinct Methodology

**SIH is not accurately described by any single existing investment category.**

The closest analogy is analyst consensus investing, but SIH extends far beyond it through three additions that transform it into something distinct:

1. **Consensus is validated, not followed.** SIH checks whether the fundamental evidence (FMP data) supports the consensus before deployment.

2. **Historical success is required.** Replay — the requirement that a similar signal configuration has historically succeeded — acts as an empirical gate that pure consensus investing lacks entirely.

3. **Portfolio discipline is a first-class constraint.** Conviction without position sizing, allocation awareness, and concentration control is just opinions. SIH enforces portfolio discipline through CW-DAS, allocation targets, and the Capital Rotation Advisor.

---

## Classification

**SIH represents Consensus Intelligence Investing (CII):**

> A multi-layer investment methodology that uses professional analyst consensus as its primary signal, validates that consensus against fundamental evidence and historical performance, and disciplines capital deployment through systematic portfolio construction.

This is distinct from consensus-following (no validation), quantitative investing (no explainability, no human authority), or multi-factor investing (no consensus hierarchy, no replay requirement).
